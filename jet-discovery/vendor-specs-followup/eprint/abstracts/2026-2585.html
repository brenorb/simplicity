Paper 2026/2585

Verifiable Post-Quantum TLS Handshake: Recognizing Implementation Errors of Cryptographic Verification Steps

Runze Zhao, Information Engineering University
Siqi Lu, Information Engineering University
Yijian Liu, Institute of Information Engineering, Chinese Academy of Sciences, School of Cyber Security, University of Chinese Academy of Sciences
Zhaoxuan Li, Institute of Information Engineering, Chinese Academy of Sciences, School of Cyber Security, University of Chinese Academy of Sciences
Kaibo Liu, Institute of Information Engineering, Chinese Academy of Sciences, School of Cyber Security, University of Chinese Academy of Sciences
Hongbo Liu, Institute of Information Engineering, Chinese Academy of Sciences, School of Cyber Security, University of Chinese Academy of Sciences
Qingjun Yuan, Information Engineering University, Xi’an Jiaotong University
Yongjuan Wang, Information Engineering University
Xianhui Lu, Institute of Information Engineering, Chinese Academy of Sciences, School of Cyber Security, University of Chinese Academy of Sciences
Abstract

The migration to post-quantum cryptography is becoming unavoidable for security protocols. Yet as the historical "goto fail" incident illustrates, deploying new primitives is not sufficient: subtle implementation bugs, especially in verification logic, can yield systems that remain fully interoperable while compromising security silently. Existing confirmation code techniques make certain errors observable, but they do not cover post-quantum signatures and provide limited protection in multi-stage handshakes. Therefore, we present a migration-oriented solution that spans both the algorithm and protocol layers. At the algorithm layer, we develop two generic confirmation-code construction frameworks for Fiat–Shamir signatures and GPV-style Hash-and-Sign signatures, instantiated with ML-DSA and Falcon. For KEMs, we further propose a protocol-friendly, KEM-level confirmation-code method, instantiated with ML-KEM, HQC, and DAWN (NTRU-based KEM). We then prove confirmation-code unpredictability for each instantiation. At the protocol layer, we design PQCD-TLS, a post-quantum TLS handshake that binds confirmation codes into chained key derivation, ensuring that any verification fault induces key mismatch and causes handshake failure. We then extend a multi-stage authenticated key-exchange model with error-injection capabilities to formalize matching correctness with implementation errors, and prove that PQCD-TLS exposes such internal errors without compromising protocol security. Our implementation evaluates 48 configurations combining confirmation code-enhanced PQC algorithms and authentication modes. Results show that handshake latency overhead remains below 1% across all tests, while turning silent verification bugs into observable handshake failures.
