Paper 2026/2485

Tulva: Practical Asynchronous Threshold FHE

Valerio Cini, Bocconi University
Antonio Guimarães, IMDEA Software
Russell W. F. Lai, Aalto University
Michał Osadnik, Aalto University
Ivy K. Y. Woo, Aalto University
Abstract

Fully homomorphic encryption (FHE) allows arbitrary computation over encrypted data, but its secret key presents a single point of failure which limits its applications. Threshold FHE patches this gap by distributing the secret key to $K$ parties, requiring a threshold $t \leq K$ of them to cooperate for decryption. We focus on the asynchronous setting where a ciphertext can be decrypted by collecting decryption shares from any $t$ parties who do not need to coordinate or even be online simultaneously. Asynchronous schemes are sought in applications such as privacy-preserving Ethereum Virtual Machines (EVM), processing of long-lived private data with quorum-based decryption and the thresholdisation of any cryptographic primitive in general. We present Tulva, a practical threshold FHE scheme which equips a base FHE scheme with an asynchronous threshold decryption mechanism in a modular way. Our scheme simultaneously (1) is compatible with virtually any base FHE scheme and leaves its ciphertexts unchanged apart from an appended random tag, (2) supports a wide range of threshold profiles $(t,K)$ and ring degrees $\geq 256$ with 6--25 bits partial decryption per plaintext bit, and (3) achieves security in the presence of partial decryption queries on challenge ciphertexts based on the standard LWE assumption. We provide an open-source implementation, benchmark its threshold decryption performance and evaluate the end-to-end costs for typical privacy-preserving EVM use cases. For 16-out-of-512 threshold and a publicly preprocessed ciphertext, computing secret-dependent partial decryption and public share combination requires only 3.7ms and 0.5ms, respectively, on commodity hardware. Compared with the state-of-the-art synchronous scheme [Zyskind et al., CCS'25], we replace their ciphertext-independent yet interactive preparation of secret-correlated randomness with a public, ciphertext-dependent 0.5s preprocessing step, eliminate interaction in the online phase while maintaining similar decryption time on the same machine, and support 32$\times$ more total users.
