Paper 2026/2627

Strongly Adaptive Asynchronous Byzantine Agreement with Linear Communication and Almost-Everywhere Termination

Renas Bacho, Ruhr University Bochum
Simon Holmgaard Kamp, Ruhr University Bochum
Julian Loss, Ruhr University Bochum
Jesper Buus Nielsen, Aarhus University
Maciej Obremski, National University of Singapore
Abstract

It is well known that Byzantine agreement requires $\Omega(t^2)$ messages against a strongly adaptive adversary that may corrupt $t=\Theta(n)$ parties and suppress a party's undelivered messages upon corruption. We circumvent this lower bound by relaxing termination: for any constant $0<\nu<1$, at least a $(1-\nu)$ fraction of the honest parties terminate, while agreement and validity hold for every honest decision. For every constant corruption gap $\delta>0$ and every $t\leq(1-\delta)n/3$, we construct binary agreement with this guarantee in authenticated asynchronous networks against strongly adaptive corruptions with message takebacks. Except with negligible probability, these guarantees hold and every honest decision carries a transferable justifier. The protocol achieves almost-everywhere termination in expected $O(1)$ rounds, expected total communication $O(n\kappa^2)$ bits, and expected maximum communication $\widetilde O(\kappa^2)$ bits over honest parties before corruption, where $\kappa$ is the computational security parameter. Our construction combines a recursively shared BLS signing key with certificate dissemination over a sparse graph. The recursive sharing is used to implement threshold signing with distinct safety and liveness thresholds. Dissemination propagates existing certificates to almost all honest parties. The construction uses a trusted setup and is secure in the random oracle model under the algebraic one-more computational Diffie--Hellman assumption. We further obtain full binary agreement with the same asymptotic communication and round complexity against adaptive corruptions without message takebacks. The extension requires neither atomic multi-send nor private communication channels.
